# Student progress report

## When to use it
At the end of an evaluation period, or when a family asks for a clear picture of their child's progress, with evidence and next steps.

## Steps
1. Gather the grades and evidence for the period: assignments, assessments, attendance and class observations, each with its date.
2. Write the concrete achievements of the period with observed examples, rather than general adjectives.
3. Identify two or three areas to improve, describing what is observed and which skill is missing.
4. Suggest realistic support at home and at school, such as guided practice or tutoring, and say who provides it.
5. Write the next steps with their dates and the next meeting or review.
6. Fill in the template `templates/student-progress-report.md` in a warm, clear and respectful tone.

## Quality criteria
- Every achievement and area to improve is backed by a dated piece of evidence.
- The language describes behaviors and learning, not labels about the person.
- Suggested support is specific and has an owner.
- The report can be read in under five minutes and contains no data about other students.

## Limits
This is a draft for the teacher's review before it is shared with the family. Do not share the report without human approval. Do not include data about other students, diagnoses, or medical or mental health information; if such concerns arise, refer the case to specialists or the institution's counselor. Do not invent grades, attendance or evidence.
