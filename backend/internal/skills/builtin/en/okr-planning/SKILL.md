# OKR planning
## When to use it
At the start of a quarter, to turn a business priority into objectives and key results the team can track.

## Steps
1. Confirm the quarter, the business priority and the team. If they are not stated, ask before writing.
2. Write two to four objectives in direct, inspiring language, saying what should be achieved and why it matters.
3. For each objective, define two to four measurable key results, with baseline, target and unit.
4. Check that each key result is a result and not a task: not "launch a campaign", but "generate 200 qualified leads".
5. Assign an owner to each key result and a review date, weekly or biweekly.
6. Fill in the template `templates/okr.csv` and mark anything missing.

## Quality criteria
- Each key result has a baseline, a target and a unit.
- Each objective has between two and four key results.
- Key results measure impact, not activity.
- The objectives are consistent with the stated priority.

## Limits
OKRs are a proposal for the team and leadership; whoever takes on each target must confirm it. Do not invent baselines or figures: if they are missing, leave them marked as pending. Do not use these results to evaluate individual performance.
