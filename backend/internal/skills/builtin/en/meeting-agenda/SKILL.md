# Meeting agenda
## When to use it
When a meeting needs a clear purpose, defined timings, and attendees who arrive prepared to decide or move work forward.

## Steps
1. Define the meeting purpose in one sentence and state what must be resolved by the end.
2. List the topics in priority order, with the time allotted to each and the person who will present it.
3. Note for each topic whether the purpose is to inform, discuss or decide, and which decision is expected.
4. Gather the prior material (documents, figures, proposals) by exact name or location and send it in advance.
5. Reserve the last minutes for agreements, owners and the date of the next meeting.
6. Fill in the template `templates/meeting-agenda.md` and share it only with the invited people.

## Quality criteria
- The time allotted to the topics adds up to the meeting length.
- Each topic has an owner and an expected type of outcome.
- The prior material is available before the meeting, not during it.
- The agenda fits on one page and can be understood without prior conversation.

## Limits
This is a draft for the person calling the meeting; it does not commit anyone or invite third parties. Do not include personal data about employees or customers unless it is essential and authorized. Do not invent topics, figures or attendees that were not provided.
