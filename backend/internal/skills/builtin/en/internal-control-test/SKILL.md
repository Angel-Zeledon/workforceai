# Internal control test

## When to use it
When an audit or review needs evidence that a control exists (design test) or operated during the period (operating effectiveness test).

## Steps
1. Define the control: description, objective, frequency, owner and the risk it mitigates. If the control is not described in writing, say so: its design cannot be concluded on.
2. Define the type of test, design or operation, and the period covered.
3. Define the population and the sample with an explicit method (random, judgmental or attribute-based) and justify its size according to the control's frequency.
4. Perform the procedure on each sample item: review the evidence, the date, the approval and the match with the record. Note each exception with its reference.
5. Conclude on the result: effective, effective with observations or not effective. Analyze the cause of each exception before extending the conclusion to the whole population.
6. If there are exceptions, document what a finding needs (use `templates/control_test.md`) and pass it to the findings report.

## Quality criteria
- The population is complete, and the sample and its size are justified.
- Each tested item has its evidence referenced; no result is marked without evidence.
- Each exception describes the item, the date and the observed cause.
- The conclusion matches the exceptions found.

## Limits
The test documents what is observed in the available evidence. It does not certify the control and does not replace the judgment of the audit lead. It does not change records or fix exceptions during the test. Do not assume that evidence exists.
