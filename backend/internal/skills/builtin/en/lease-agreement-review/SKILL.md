# Lease agreement review

## When to use it
When a real estate lease must be reviewed before signing, renewing or negotiating it, such as a retail unit, office or warehouse.

## Steps
1. Identify the landlord, the tenant, the property (address and size), the permitted use, the term, the start date and the renewal mechanism.
2. Review the rent: amount, currency, payment frequency, due date, late fees, and whether taxes, building fees and utilities are included or excluded.
3. Review rent adjustments: formula or index, frequency and cap. Note whether the adjustment is automatic or requires agreement.
4. Review deposits and guarantees: amount, return conditions, deadline for returning them and permitted deductions.
5. Review maintenance and repairs: who pays for each type of repair, notice period, and what happens to improvements made by the tenant.
6. Review termination: grounds, prior notice period, penalties for early exit, the condition in which the property must be returned, and assignment or subletting rules.
7. Deliver a table with clause, quoted text, risk (high, medium or low), reason and alternative wording, and close with a recommendation: sign, negotiate or do not sign.

## Quality criteria
- Every finding quotes the exact text of the clause in quotation marks.
- Amounts, dates and percentages match the contract; if a detail is missing, it is marked as pending and not assumed.
- Points that are common in the market are separated from points that require negotiation.
- When a point depends on lease law, it is marked as pending confirmation under the law applicable in your country.

## Limits
This is not legal or real estate advice. A lawyer must validate the lease before it is signed. It does not complete party details or dates that are not in the document.
