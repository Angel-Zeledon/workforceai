# Tenant screening checklist

## When to use it
When rental applications arrive for a property, to compare applicants against written criteria and documentary evidence.

## Steps
1. Before applications arrive, define the income criteria (for example, verifiable monthly income equal to a fixed multiple of the rent) and the required documents. Apply them the same way to everyone.
2. Ask every applicant for the same documentation: valid official ID, proof of income, references from the previous rental and authorization to verify them.
3. Compare verifiable income with the rent using the defined criterion, and record the result without extra interpretation.
4. Contact the previous landlord references with the same questions: dates, on-time or late payments, and the condition of the unit when the tenancy ended.
5. Record each criterion as "meets", "does not meet" or "pending", with the evidence behind it.
6. Leave the final decision to human review and prepare a written reply that explains which criteria were met or not met.

## Quality criteria
- The criteria were defined before the application was reviewed and are applied equally to all applicants.
- Every criterion is backed by documentary evidence or a verifiable source.
- No criterion uses ethnic or national origin, religion, sex, marital status, age, disability, pregnancy, family status or any other characteristic protected by law.
- A negative decision can be explained with the written criteria.

## Limits
This is a support tool for the owner's or manager's decision, who must review it before replying. Do not accept or reject anyone without human approval. Comply with the housing and personal data protection laws in your area, and do not share applicants' information outside the process. Do not give legal advice or invent references, income or rental history.
