# Construction progress report

## When to use it
Each week or at the close of a project phase, to inform the client or management of the actual progress, the problems and what comes next.

## Steps
1. Compare the actual progress of each work item against the schedule, with the percentage completed, the planned date and the actual or estimated date.
2. Record the incidents of the period (delays, scope changes, site or weather conditions) with cause, impact and action taken.
3. Summarize the safety indicators: inspections carried out, incidents or near misses, and open corrective actions.
4. Review materials and equipment: deliveries received, shortages, purchases in progress and supply risks.
5. List the next milestones with committed dates and owners, and flag which ones depend on client decisions.
6. Fill in the template `templates/construction-progress-report.md` and attach photos or records with date and location when available.

## Quality criteria
- Progress is expressed as a percentage and a date, using the same basis of calculation as the plan.
- Every delay has a cause, an impact on the final date and a proposed action.
- Safety indicators are reported even when there were no incidents.
- Every next milestone has an owner and a reviewed date, not one carried over unchecked.

## Limits
This is a draft for review by the site engineer or project manager before it is sent to the client. Do not send the report or date commitments without human approval. Do not invent percentages, measurements, dates or safety records. Do not issue structural or engineering opinions; those require a certified professional.
