# Contract redline summary

## When to use it
When a new version of a contract, a counter-draft or a renegotiated document arrives, and the effect of each change must be understood before replying.

## Steps
1. Confirm which version is the base and which is the new one, with date and file name, so the comparison is not reversed.
2. Compare section by section, including definitions, annexes and cross-references. Mark additions, deletions and wording changes.
3. Ignore changes that are only formatting or numbering, and say in the summary that they were left out.
4. For each substantive change, give the previous and the new text in quotation marks, who benefits, and the effect on amounts, deadlines, risks or liabilities.
5. Classify each change as acceptable, negotiate or reject, with its reason.
6. Close with the key changes and the questions to resolve with the other party.

## Quality criteria
- Each substantive change quotes the text before and after.
- No change goes unreported, including those that affect a clause indirectly through a definition or cross-reference.
- The impact is stated in concrete figures (amounts, days, responsibilities), not in adjectives.
- Changes of substance are distinguished from changes that only reorder text.

## Limits
It does not replace the legal review of the final version. Accepting or rejecting changes is decided by a person with authority to negotiate.
