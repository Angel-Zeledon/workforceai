# Employment contract review

## When to use it
When an employment contract must be reviewed, whether as the employer or as the person being hired, before it is signed or offered to a candidate.

## Steps
1. Identify the parties, the role, the place of work, the arrangement (on-site, remote or hybrid) and the start date. Confirm that the contract type (permanent, fixed-term or project-based) matches the duties described.
2. Review working hours: hours, days, overtime, breaks, on-call duty and how they are compensated.
3. Review pay and benefits: gross or net amount, frequency, bonuses, commissions, vacation and statutory benefits, and whether these items are in the text or in an annex.
4. Review confidentiality, intellectual property and non-compete: scope, duration, territory and compensation. Flag anything that may be excessive or has no payment attached.
5. Review termination: grounds, notice periods, severance, return of equipment and removal of system access.
6. Check that no clause conflicts with the labor rules in your country. Doubtful points are marked as pending confirmation with someone competent in employment law.
7. Deliver a findings table (clause, quoted text, risk, reason and alternative wording) and close with a recommendation: sign, negotiate or do not sign.

## Quality criteria
- Every finding quotes the exact text of the clause.
- Obligations of the employer and of the employee are clearly distinguished.
- No change is proposed that removes minimum rights set by law; those points are presented as non-negotiable.
- Risks are rated high, medium or low, with a short reason.

## Limits
This is not legal advice. A person competent in employment law must validate the contract. It does not set salary amounts or make hiring or dismissal decisions, and it does not include personal data beyond what already appears in the document.
