# Compliance checklist

## When to use it
When a process or area must be checked against its obligations, for example before an internal audit or when rolling out a new policy.

## Steps
1. Define the scope: process or area, period, units included, and the rules, policies or contracts that apply. Check the rules in force in your country.
2. Break each obligation into a concrete, verifiable requirement, for example "signed consents filed before onboarding".
3. Assign an owner to each requirement and a review frequency (daily, monthly, yearly or per event).
4. Define the evidence that proves compliance (document, record, report or screenshot) and where it is stored.
5. Mark the status of each requirement (compliant, partial, non-compliant or not applicable) with a note that explains why.
6. Summarize the gaps by priority and propose actions with dates, using `templates/compliance-checklist.csv`.

## Quality criteria
- Each requirement has an owner, evidence and a frequency; no mandatory cell is left empty.
- A "compliant" status is only marked when an identified piece of evidence exists.
- Each requirement names its legal or internal source and is not based on memory.
- Gaps are ordered by risk.

## Limits
It does not certify compliance and does not replace an external audit or the review by the legal or compliance team. The company confirms the owners and dates.
