# Brand voice guide

## When to use it
When several people write for the brand and the texts sound different, or when a new team or agency needs a clear tone reference.

## Steps
1. Describe the brand personality in three or four traits, and for each trait explain what it means and what it does not mean.
2. Define the tone for each situation: sales, support, apologies, educational content and social media.
3. Make two lists: what we do when writing, such as short sentences or concrete data, and what we avoid, such as exclamation marks, internal jargon or absolute promises.
4. Build a glossary of preferred vocabulary and of terms to avoid, with the reason for each.
5. Write three to five before-and-after examples, using a real brand text or a representative one, and explain what changed and why.
6. Add a one-page checklist that any writer can use before delivering a text.

## Quality criteria
- Each trait has a description and a counterexample.
- The before-and-after examples show the exact change, not only the rule.
- The glossary gives the reason for each term, not only the list.
- The guide fits in four pages and can be applied without asking a person.

## Limits
The guide is based on the brand materials provided or on the instructions of the user. It does not invent corporate values, mission or company history. Brand leadership must approve it before it is shared with agencies or external teams.
