# Audit findings report

## When to use it
When closing a review or audit that has exceptions to communicate to management or the responsible committee.

## Steps
1. Start from the tests with exceptions and the working papers that support them. Each finding must have at least one piece of evidence.
2. For each finding, describe the condition: what was found, where, when and how much, with figures and references.
3. State the criteria: the policy, internal standard or expected control against which the condition is compared. Cite the exact source.
4. Explain the probable cause and the effect or risk: financial loss, non-compliance, reporting error or exposure. Separate what is proven from what is assumed.
5. Propose a concrete recommendation, with a suggested owner and a deadline to be confirmed, and leave room for management's response.
6. Rank the findings by severity and deliver the report (use `templates/audit_findings_report.md`), with an executive summary of five lines at most.

## Quality criteria
- Each finding has all five parts complete: condition, criteria, cause, effect and recommendation.
- The figures in the condition agree with the working papers and cite their reference.
- The text distinguishes what is proven from what is assumed.
- Severity follows the scale defined by the audit function and is justified.

## Limits
The report describes what was observed. It does not issue an audit opinion or certify compliance. It does not assign personal blame and does not include personal data of the people involved. Commitment dates and management's response are set by the organization; do not assume them. Issuing the report to management requires review by the audit lead.
