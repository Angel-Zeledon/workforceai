# Price and margin analysis

## When to use it
When a product or service price must be set or reviewed, when a promotion is being evaluated, or when you need to know how many units are required to cover costs.

## Steps
1. Define the product or service, the unit of sale, the net price (after discounts) and the analysis period.
2. Separate the variable cost per unit (materials, direct labor, commissions, shipping) from the period's fixed costs (rent, fixed salaries, software, depreciation).
3. Calculate the contribution margin per unit: net price minus variable costs. Also express the margin as a percentage of the net price.
4. Calculate the break-even point: period fixed costs divided by the contribution margin per unit gives units; multiply that result by the net price to get revenue.
5. Compare scenarios: price change, cost change and maximum allowed discount. Show the margin and break-even point of each.
6. Deliver a summary with one table per product and the conclusions in plain language. Do not present a final price as a decision; the person who sets prices approves it.

## Quality criteria
- The margin per unit is calculated from the net price and variable costs, not from the list price.
- Fixed costs allocated to products add up to the period total, and each allocation is explained.
- Each scenario shows its break-even units and revenue with the formula used.
- Assumptions (volume, costs and period) are written at the start of the analysis.

## Limits
This is a supporting analysis. It does not set or publish prices, send quotes or change price lists. If cost data is missing, it is marked as an assumption and not filled in with estimates presented as facts. It excludes sales taxes unless requested; verify their treatment under the applicable rules.
