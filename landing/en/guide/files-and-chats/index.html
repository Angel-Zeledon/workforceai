Chats and Files: pick up conversations and find what was produced

Chats keeps what you discuss with your agents and with the people in your organization, plus one row for every request you make. Files gathers the documents your agents create and the ones you upload, with versions and a trash so nothing gets lost. Here you learn to find, write, upload and recover.

Find a conversation#

The Chats list has three groups: “Agents”, which starts with “The whole company” (everything you say to your agents, in order) and then one chat per agent; “People”, with the chats between people; and “Requests”, with one row for everything you asked the office for.

  1. Open “Chats” in the side menu.
  2. Press “The whole company” to see everything, or an agent to see only its chat. Agents with recent activity come first and a number shows unread messages.
  3. To search, type in “Search agents, people and requests…”. The list is replaced by the results, grouped into Agents, People and Requests.
  4. Press the “Clear search” X to go back to the list.

Write to the office or to an agent#

  1. Pick “The whole company” or an agent in the list.
  2. Type your message in the box at the bottom and send it.
  3. Check under your message which agents it was sent to (“Sent to”): in The whole company, the office routes it to the right agent; in an agent's chat, it goes straight to it.

Tip

In an agent's chat, “See in the office” takes you to its desk in the 3D view.

Talk to the people on your team#

The “People” group gathers chats between people: the organization-wide channel, direct messages and groups, which you can also add agents to.

  1. Press “New” in the “People” group.
  2. Choose “Direct message” and a person, or “Group”, a name and its members (people and agents).
  3. Press “Create” and write. Use @ to mention people or agents.

Note

Admins can also create “Office” and “Project” channels. With the Read only role you can read chats but not write.

Open a request and its report#

Each request has its row in “Requests”, with its status and who is doing it. Pressing it opens its panel next to the chat, with five tabs: “Summary”, “Tasks”, “Files”, “Report” and “How they worked on it”, where you read, without writing, what the agents said to each other while working on it.

  1. Press a request in the list, or “Open request” on its message in the chat.
  2. In “Summary” you see “What was asked”, the “Status” and “Who is doing it”. If it comes from a project, press “Open project”; if it is waiting for you, press “Go to Pending”.
  3. Under “Organize”, use “Rename” to give it a title you recognize.
  4. Press “Archive” to take it out of the list; “See archived” shows them and “Restore” brings them back.
  5. Press “Delete” and confirm with “Yes, delete” to erase the request's conversation.

Warning

Deleting erases the request's conversation for good along with its messages. Only an Admin or the Owner can do it.

Browse your files#

Files holds the documents your agents create and the ones you upload. You start at “Home”, with four root folders:

  1. Open “Files” in the side menu.
  2. Press a folder to enter it. The table shows “Name”, “Last change”, “Version”, “Size” and “Updated”.
  3. Press a file to open it in the right panel. Text, CSV, Markdown, JSON and YAML files are previewed there; anything else opens with “Download”.
  4. Use the folder path at the top to go back: press a segment to go up to that folder, or “Home” to return to the root.
FolderWhat it holdsWhich agents read it
Agent desksOne folder per agent with its workEach agent, its own
ProjectsOne folder per project, with its deliverables and inputsThe agents assigned to that project

Tip

The “Last change” column tells you whether an agent, a person or the system made it.

Each department's folders#

Files shows an “Offices” root with one folder per department (for example Accounting). Each one starts with the subfolders that fit its profession (in Accounting: Clients, Suppliers, Taxes and more). If there is no template for the profession, it starts with Documents and Policies. If the department is private, its folder shows a lock.

Members of a department can edit. Someone who is not a member of an open department can only read. Someone who is not a member of a private department sees nothing, even as an administrator. A department's agents work in its folder without asking for approval, never touch other departments' folders, and can never do more than the person they work for.

  1. Open “Files” in the side menu.
  2. Click “Offices” and choose your department's folder.
  3. Upload files or open the subfolders to keep them in order.

Tip

Files you attach in a chat are saved by default in the Attachments folder of the department of the agent you chat with, in one subfolder per day, when you are a member of that department (if you are not, or that agent has no department, they go to Shared > Chat); you can pick another folder.

Upload your own files#

  1. Enter a folder: you cannot upload at “Home”.
  2. Press “Upload” and choose one or more files, or drag them from your computer onto the screen.
  3. Drop the files when you see the notice with the destination folder and wait for them to finish uploading.

Note

By default, each file can be up to 25 MB. Which agents can read it depends on the folder, as the table above shows.

Search by name, content or meaning#

Type in “Search files…” and press Enter. The search looks at names and the text inside files, and each result shows a snippet. Press a result to go to its folder, or “Close” to return to the list.

Turn on “By meaning” to search passages by what they mean, also inside PDFs and long documents. Each result shows the page and the snippet.

Tip

If search by meaning is not available, the app tells you and shows results by words.

Recover an earlier version or a deleted file#

Every time a person or an agent changes a file, a version is saved, and “Move to trash” only sends it to the “Trash”, where you can restore it.

  1. Open a file and look at its “Version history”. Press a version (v1, v2…) to see how it was.
  2. Press “Restore” if you prefer that version. A new version is created with that content; history is never erased.
  3. To recover a deleted file, press “Trash” and then “Restore” next to the file. It goes back to its folder.
  4. Press “Trash” again to return to your files.

Note

Moving a file to the trash requires the Admin or Owner role.
