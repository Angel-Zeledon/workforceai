Approvals and security: you stay in control

Your AI employees prepare and propose, but the delicate decisions are yours. This guide shows where you decide, how you limit spending and permissions, and how to halt everything with one click.

When does an employee ask for permission?

When an agent wants to do something that needs your go-ahead, such as sending an email or creating an event, a notice appears and the agent waits at the approvals desk in the office. Nothing continues without your decision.

Each notice shows who is asking, which action it is, the details and a risk level. An agent can ask for approval but never give it: a person always does, whatever the agent's role.

If your role can see an approval but not decide it, the card says so and hides the buttons.

How do I approve or reject an action?

  1. Open the pending approvals tray. It unfolds by itself when a new one arrives, and you can hide or show it whenever you like.
  2. Read the card's title, details and risk.
  3. Press Approve to let the agent continue, or Reject to stop that action.
  4. Away from the office? Open the Approvals screen on your phone and decide there. You can turn on notifications, which only show the title and the risk; you make the decision inside, with your session.
Tip. Some approvals need two different people; the mobile card shows how many have been received.

Can I stop approving repetitive things?

For internal, reversible actions in a project, the card offers a checkbox: Always allow this kind of action in this project. You can extend it to the project's other agents and revoke it later in the project's Approvals tab.

Sending, publishing, paying, deleting and running code will always ask for approval. Emails also wait a short window after you approve them so you can cancel the send; once sent, it cannot be undone.

How much will it cost, and how do I set a cap?

Before running a request that may touch connections with write access, the plan review shows an estimated cost (with its likely upper value) and the approximate time. It is an estimate, not an invoice.

  1. Open Settings and go into Security.
  2. Scroll to Spend limits. The table shows the scope (organization, employee, project, connection or tool), the period, what has been used and the limit.
  3. Check the When reached column: each limit can warn, pause and ask, or block.
  4. In Connections, the Limits tab adds uses per day, writes per day and a monthly budget for each connection.

Where are my keys stored?

The keys and secrets for your connections are sent once and stored encrypted on the server, in a vault. The screen never shows them to you again.

The AI model never sees them either: tools are run by the backend, not by the model. With real AI, the text of tasks does travel to the model provider you configure, so check their terms before using sensitive data. We hold no security certifications.

How do I decide what each employee can do?

Connecting an account is not granting access: each permission is assigned separately, following least privilege. Today the Gmail flow is tested in simulation; with real accounts it is still less tested.

  1. Open Settings and go into Connections.
  2. Connect an account and follow the wizard: name, connection type and what it will be able to do.
  3. Open the Permissions by employee tab. Rows are employees and columns are connections; click a cell to view or change its permissions.
  4. Write permissions are granted separately and, if there is more than one admin, another person must approve the grant.
  5. Check What each employee can do and the Usage log to see what happened.

How do I use the Security center?

Security and control gathers the office's brakes. From there you can:

  1. Turn on Global read-only mode: employees keep reading and proposing, but send and change nothing.
  2. Pause an employee, or stop one tool for everyone without touching the rest. A paused employee resumes where they left off.
  3. Set the Operating hours: outside them, new work waits in plain sight and resumes when the office opens. Approvals and the emergency switch keep working.
  4. Review the Unusual activity alerts: simple rules that warn you, for example, about a spend spike or many rejected approvals. On their own they block nothing, unless you turn on automatic freezing (off by default).

How do I stop everything in an emergency?

The Stop everything button is in Settings and in the Security center. Nothing is deleted. Any admin can stop; only the owner can lift the lock.

  1. Press Stop everything and pick a level: Everything stopped (nothing new starts and connection use is blocked, but accounts stay connected) or Full lockdown (also suspends all connections and cancels pending sends).
  2. Type a reason if you like and confirm with Yes, stop everything.
  3. To resume, the owner opens Security, writes a reason and presses Lift lock. Pending actions will ask for approval again.
