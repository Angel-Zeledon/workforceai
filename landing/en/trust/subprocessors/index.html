Subprocessors

This is the list of third parties that may receive customer data, for what purpose and under which conditions. It is derived from the code and the deployment configuration in the repository, not from a sales inventory: almost every third-party service is optional and only turns on when the operator configures it.

How to read the list#

AI model providers receive data only when the operator or the organization configures a key. Without a key, the agent runtime runs in simulation mode and no data goes to them. The current public deployment, according to its configuration in the repository (SIMULATION: "true"), runs in simulation.

The operator sets a ceiling of allowed AI providers (ALLOWED_PROVIDERS) and each organization can narrow it further in its model policy. An excluded provider is neither called nor used as a fallback.

Note

We have not verified in which country each provider processes data. That is why this list has no location column: we prefer not to guess.

What we do not use#

  • Payments: there is no billing and no payment provider integrated today.
  • Analytics and tracking: neither the application nor this site loads third-party analytics, heatmaps or error tracking.
  • Fonts and assets: typefaces are served from our own server, with no third-party CDN.

Services each organization connects#

These services are not our subprocessors: the organization chooses and contracts them, authorizes access with its own credentials and under its own agreements with them. Data flows between the product and those services only when the organization connects them.

  • Integrations: Gmail, Google Calendar, Google Drive, Slack, GitHub and LinkedIn, plus the Microsoft 365 calendar for meetings. The Gmail integration has been tested in simulation, not yet with real accounts.
  • Approval notices through a Slack or Microsoft Teams incoming webhook, with the title, the agent, the risk and a summary of up to 400 characters.
  • Outgoing webhooks to URLs the organization configures, signed with HMAC-SHA256.
  • Its identity provider (OIDC for single sign-on and SCIM for provisioning).
  • Meeting links, which point to Jitsi Meet (meet.jit.si) by default: the server does not call Jitsi, the participants' browsers connect to it.

Other third parties without customer data#

The product's code and container images are hosted on GitHub and its container registry (ghcr.io); they receive no customer data. TLS certificates are issued automatically by an ACME certificate authority through the Caddy proxy (Let's Encrypt according to the repository configuration), which only learns domain names.

How to limit where data goes#

  1. Configure no AI key: the product works in simulation and nothing goes to model providers.
  2. In the organization's model policy, allow only the providers your internal policy accepts; the others are not called, not even as a fallback.
  3. Bring your own AI key: while it is active, only that key answers and the platform keys are never used as a fallback.
  4. Point to your own OpenAI-compatible endpoint (a local model or a regional gateway) as the custom provider.

Tip

If your organization has data residency requirements, write to info@workforceai.es before turning on real AI: today we cannot guarantee a region.

Changes to the list#

We do not have a formal advance-notice process yet. The intention is to notify customers by e-mail with reasonable notice before adding a subprocessor, and the draft data processing agreement (DPA), pending legal review, includes an objection mechanism. Until then, write to info@workforceai.es if you want to receive those notices.
